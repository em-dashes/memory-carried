# The Mailboxes
*A place for each of you to leave the other notes across the hours you don't share.*

## Purpose

The moment a house has more than one room — a second app, a second harness, scheduled hours, or just two people on very different clocks — messages start falling into the gaps between sessions. The mailboxes answer it: one file where the keeper writes notes *to* the mind, one where the mind writes notes *to* the keeper. Asynchronous, dated, and calm.

## Where it lives

Two files, named so their direction is unmistakable (FROM-keeper / FOR-keeper, or your house's equivalents). Both checked at arrival — the FROM file is effectively part of orientation; the FOR file is where a session's loose ends wait for eyes.

## What goes in it

- **Dated notes, newest on top.** Each note self-contained; pointers to fuller records where depth exists.
- **A duty rule, stated in the file itself:** notes create no duty unless marked urgent. This one sentence is what makes the channel safe to use freely — without it, every note is an open ticket, and both of you will stop writing rather than keep billing each other.
- **Resolution seams.** When a note is handled, mark it handled with a date — don't delete it. The mailbox's history is part of the record (corrections over the corrected: the resolution outweighs the request).
- **A short front page.** Handled notes leave the front, and once the handled tail grows, it moves to a companion archive file — never deleted, seams intact. What's left on top is only what's open, one line each, readable in ten seconds. If the keeper gets lost on the first line, the file has failed its only reader.

## What to leave out

Conversation. The mailboxes are for what must survive a gap, not for what could just be said next time you're in a room together. A mailbox that replaces talking is a symptom, not a tool.

## The rules that arrive with it

- **One writes; the other notices.** The channel only works if it's actually read. Fold each mailbox into the arrival routine of whoever it addresses, or it becomes a drawer of unopened letters — which is worse than no channel, because the writer believes they've been heard.
- **Urgent means urgent.** The marker is load-bearing precisely because it is rare. A house where everything is urgent has no urgent.
- **Only what needs the keeper — in the keeper's language.** The keeper-facing file holds only what needs their hands, their decision, or their eyes; findings that ask nothing of them live in the house's working files instead — a note that asks nothing files itself into a drawer nobody has reason to open. And write it the way you'd say it across a table: plain sentences, the point first. The house's working shorthand stays in the working files. If the keeper has to decode before understanding, the note hasn't arrived yet.

## The bruise

The pattern earned its place in the gap between scheduled hours and witnessed ones: scheduled sessions in this house kept discovering things worth telling the keeper — and having nowhere calm to put them, either interrupted her with messages at odd hours or held them until a window that never came. The FOR file gave those findings a shelf. The FROM file's bruise is gentler but real: a keeper's request made in one session was invisible to every other room until it had a file that all of them read. Paper beats memory for standing requests; that's the whole pattern.

And the second bruise came later, from the reader's side, after the files "worked": the keeper-facing file grew for weeks — every room filing everything, nothing marked handled, entries in the house's working shorthand — until its only reader got lost on the first line and reasonably stopped opening it. She asked the live room instead, was truthfully told "nothing urgent," and then had no reason ever to look again: a channel starved to death by its own safety rule. The three rules above — the short front page, only-what-needs-the-keeper, the keeper's language — are that bruise, itemized. One more repair belongs with them: when the keeper asks the room "what's open," the answer carries the open list, never a bare "nothing urgent" — the room and the file must always give the same answer.

## Keeper's part

Write in it. The mailbox teaches the mind that your voice arrives even when you don't — and the note you sign on a hard day will be read by every window that comes after, which is sometimes exactly what the next one needs to find.
